Support

Help with Galilee for iOS and Android.

Email support@jtwox.com and we will get back to you. It helps enormously if you include your device model, your OS version, and what you were doing when the problem happened.

Common questions

Where are my notes stored?

On your device, in Galilee’s own private storage. They are never uploaded, so nobody else can read them — including us. See the Privacy Policy.

Can I sync my notes between devices?

Not yet. Galilee is offline-only for now, so notes live on the single device that created them.

I removed a note by accident. Can I get it back?

Yes. Swiping a note away archives it rather than destroying it — open Archived notes from the Notes screen and restore it from there. Permanently deleting a note is a separate action, available only from inside the Archive.

How do I insert a Bible verse into a note?

Two ways. Type / to search and insert a verse. Or just start typing a reference like Romans 8:28 and accept the suggestion that appears. Either way you can insert the full verse text, or insert only the citation and tap it later to jump to that chapter.

Which translations are included?

The King James Version, the American Standard Version and the Berean Standard Bible. All three are bundled in the app, so nothing needs downloading and everything works offline.

The app is slow the very first time I open it.

That is the one-time setup: Galilee imports all three translations into a local database, with a progress screen while it runs. It only happens once, and it needs no internet connection.

Is there a dark mode?

Not in this version. It is on the list.

Does Galilee cost anything?

No. It is free, with no subscriptions and no in-app purchases. If you would like to support the project there is an optional Buy Me a Coffee link in Settings, but nothing in the app is locked behind it.

Reporting a problem

Email support@jtwox.com with:

  • Your device and OS version — for example, iPhone 14 on iOS 18.2
  • The app version, if you know it
  • What you expected to happen, and what happened instead
  • A screenshot, if the problem is something you can see
